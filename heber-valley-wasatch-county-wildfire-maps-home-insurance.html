Heber Valley and Wasatch County homes: wildfire maps, growth, and insurance

Two wildfire maps, the largest Utah wildfire of 2024, and a fast-growing housing stock, from county, state, and news sources.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published November 23, 2026. Sources checked November 23, 2026. See our editorial policy.

Heber City, Midway, and the rest of Wasatch County sit next to Summit County, where a U.S. Senate report found the steepest rise in Utah home insurance non-renewals. Wasatch County has its own story: two different wildfire maps, the largest Utah wildfire of 2024, and fast growth in newer and second homes. This page lays out what public sources say. If you have already received a non-renewal notice, start with our dropped or non-renewed page.

What we can and cannot say about non-renewals here

The Senate Budget Committee staff report we cite for Summit County lists Summit County in its county tables but does not list Wasatch County (Senate Budget Committee, Next to Fall, Tables 1 and 2). Figures for Wasatch County circulate on secondary websites, but we only publish rates we can trace to a primary source, so this page gives none. For the Summit County figures, see Park City and Summit County non-renewals.

Two wildfire maps, two different jobs

Wasatch County has its own Wildland Urban Interface (WUI) map. The county's map, adopted May 3, 2017, marks wildland fire areas around Heber City and nearby towns (Wasatch County Wildland Fire Interface Map), and the Wasatch County Fire District tells owners to look up an address on the county's online map, where anything shaded orange is in the WUI (Wasatch County Fire District, Am I in the WUI?). The county's 2026 General Plan, adopted July 15, 2026, says the parts of the county not shown on its map of the WUI's extent are all in the WUI, and calls for higher fire-resistant construction standards there (Wasatch County General Plan 2026, Map 2-2 and Goal 2.5).

Towns adopt and adjust that local map. Wallsburg's town council, for example, passed a resolution on December 4, 2025 amending its adoption of the county fire district's WUI map to say no WUI areas are identified inside Wallsburg's town limits; the meeting record describes the map as a guide for rules such as fireworks and building codes (Wallsburg Town Council meeting packet, Resolution 2025-11).

That local map is not the one insurers use. The state Division of Forestry, Fire and State Lands (FFSL) explains that a local WUI zone is drawn by counties and cities to enforce the Utah WUI Code, while FFSL's separate high-risk WUI boundary decides where the state fee applies (FFSL, WUI Zone vs. WUI Boundary). Under Utah Code 31A-22-1310, an insurer deciding whether a property is high-risk WUI property may only use the state boundary. Our explainer on Utah's high-risk WUI boundary covers the state map, and our guide to checking your home's wildfire risk walks through looking up an address on the Utah Wildfire Risk Assessment Portal.

The Yellow Lake Fire

The Yellow Lake Fire started September 28, 2024, about 10 miles up State Route 35 from Francis, across the Wasatch County line. KPCW reported it was the largest wildfire in Utah that year at about 33,000 acres, double the next biggest, and that it was accidentally started by logging equipment. The Forest Service said 442 structures were threatened and none were lost; most of the 12 evacuation zones covered recreation areas and seasonal cabins (KPCW, January 9, 2025). The Forest Service also warned that loss of vegetation means erosion is likely a threat to structures near the burn scar. The Utah Insurance Department lists flood, earth movement, and mudslide among perils typically not covered on a homeowners policy (Utah Insurance Department, Homeowner's Insurance); see flood coverage in Utah.

Newer homes, higher prices, more second homes

The county's General Plan reports about 14,068 housing units in 2025, with growth concentrated near Heber City and the Jordanelle Basin, and says about 60% of the county's homes were built within the last 25 years. It cites a Wasatch County median home sales price of $1.2 million for the year ending 2025, from the Park City Board of Realtors, and Census figures showing 22.40% of housing units vacant, and it says short-term rentals and second homes have increased significantly in resort-adjacent areas (Wasatch County General Plan 2026, Housing chapter).

Two public-source points connect to that. First, the Utah Insurance Department says replacement cost is different from market value, which includes land (Utah Insurance Department, Homeowner's Insurance), so a high sale price does not tell you the dwelling limit; our guide on why dwelling limits change at renewal explains how the limit is set. Second, the NAIC says homeowners insurance is not designed to cover most business uses of a home (NAIC, A Consumer's Guide to Home Insurance). How a second home or a short-term rental is treated depends on the policy, so that is a question for your insurer or agent.

Fire protection class and earthquakes

In March 2024 KPCW listed the Wasatch County Fire District's ISO public protection rating as 4/4X, alongside Park City at 3/3X (KPCW, March 20, 2024). Our article on fire protection classes explains what those numbers mean for rating.

On earthquakes, a 1988 U.S. Bureau of Reclamation study for Jordanelle Dam, published by the Utah Geological Survey, describes a magnitude 4.3 Heber City earthquake in 1972 on the eastern edge of Heber Valley, tied to a fault with no surface expression, and found no evidence of late Quaternary surface displacement on faults in Heber Valley itself (USBR Seismotectonic Report 88-6). That is an older dam study, not a current hazard rating for any home. Separately, the Utah Insurance Department says earthquakes are generally not covered under a homeowners policy (Utah Insurance Department); see does homeowners insurance cover earthquakes in Utah?

Questions to bring to your insurer or an agent

  • Is my property inside the state's high-risk WUI boundary, and did that affect my renewal?
  • If my renewal changed because of wildfire risk, what facts was it based on?
  • How does my policy treat a second home, a vacant period, or short-term rental use?
  • Which fire protection class is my address rated under?
