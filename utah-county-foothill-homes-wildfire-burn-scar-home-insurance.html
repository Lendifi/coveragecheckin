Utah County foothill homes: wildfire evacuations, burn scars, and your policy

Facts from the Utah Geological Survey, the Utah Insurance Department, news reports, and the Census Bureau, plus questions to ask an agent.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published November 2, 2026. Sources checked November 2, 2026. See our editorial policy.

In 2018 and again in 2020, wildfires forced thousands of homes in Utah County's foothill neighborhoods to evacuate, and in 2002 subdivisions south of Provo were damaged by mud and debris that came off a burned slope. Wildfire, evacuation costs, and post-fire flooding can each be handled differently by a homeowners policy. If you want time to ask about them before your policy renews, our free renewal check can send you a reminder ahead of that date.

To look up your own address, see how to check your Utah home's wildfire risk. For the state boundary insurers must use to decide whether a property is high-risk wildland urban interface, see what Utah's high-risk WUI boundary is.

2020: the Knolls Fire in Saratoga Springs

On June 28, 2020, the Knolls Fire, which started in an area known as the Knolls, swept up to fences and backyards in Saratoga Springs. The city evacuated about 3,100 homes and about 13,000 residents, roughly a third of the city's population. One home outside city limits was destroyed and 12 houses in the city had some damage, according to the city's spokesman. The Salt Lake Tribune reported that the evacuation was believed to be the largest ever ordered in Utah for a wildfire, larger than the 2010 Machine Gun Fire in Herriman (more than 1,650 homes evacuated, three destroyed) (The Salt Lake Tribune, June 29, 2020). The same weekend, the Traverse Fire on the ridge between Lehi and Draper prompted evacuations in both cities; officials said illegal fireworks started it, and no homes were lost.

2018: Pole Creek and Bald Mountain

In 2018 the Pole Creek and Bald Mountain fires were both started by lightning around Mount Nebo. The Tribune later put the Pole Creek evacuation at 2,000 homes in Woodland Hills and Elk Ridge (The Salt Lake Tribune). The two fires merged and burned about 120,000 acres. No homes or lives were lost, and the Forest Service spent at least $30 million fighting them. The Tribune reported that some slopes burned badly enough that damaged watersheds could take years to recover and could shed debris flows into Woodland Hills and Elk Ridge (The Salt Lake Tribune, June 16, 2019).

After the fire: debris flows in Santaquin and Spring Lake

The Utah Geological Survey documented what that can look like. The 2001 Mollie Fire burned about 8,000 acres on Dry Mountain, about 18 miles south of Provo. On September 12, 2002, a short thunderstorm that dropped 0.27 inches of rain on the burned slopes set off debris flows that reached subdivisions in Spring Lake and Santaquin. In Santaquin one flow moved cars, broke through a house wall, and entered other houses through basement windows and doors; most of the damage came from sediment and water flowing into houses (UGS, fire-related debris flows damage houses). The UGS noted that debris-flow and flood hazards existed on those alluvial fans before the fire.

Flood, earth movement, and mudslide are on the Utah Insurance Department's list of perils typically not covered by a homeowners policy; coverage for them can come from an endorsement or a separate policy (Utah Insurance Department, homeowners insurance). How your own policy treats mud or water that comes down after a fire is a question for your agent or insurer.

Evacuation costs

The Insurance Department's wildfire guide says Additional Living Expenses (Loss of Use) coverage under standard homeowners policies used in Utah can help during a mandatory wildfire evacuation even if the home has not burned, but that triggers and timelines vary by carrier. It says purely voluntary evacuations are out-of-pocket expenses, and it suggests saving copies of official evacuation orders and itemized receipts (Utah Insurance Department, wildfire preparation and evacuation guide).

Newer homes at the edge of open land

Utah County's housing is young. Census Bureau estimates (2020 to 2024) put the county's median year built at 2001, compared with 1992 statewide, and 108,859 of about 211,066 housing units (51.6%) were built in 2000 or later, compared with 38.6% statewide (ACS 2024 5-year, table B25034, via Census Reporter). We computed the shares from those counts. Newer construction does not by itself tell you how close a home sits to brush or a burn scar; our guide on checking your home's wildfire risk walks through the state's free mapping tools.

Questions to bring to an agent

  • What does my policy pay toward living costs during a mandatory evacuation, and what proof does my insurer ask for?
  • How does my policy word the flood, earth movement, and mudslide exclusions, and how would mud or debris from a burned slope be treated?
  • Is my address inside the state's high-risk WUI boundary, and does my insurer use it at renewal?
  • If I want flood coverage, when would it take effect?

Before you share details with anyone selling a policy, you can verify that the agent is licensed in Utah. Rural fire service can also affect pricing; see what a fire protection class is.
