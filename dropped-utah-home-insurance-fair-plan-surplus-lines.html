Dropped and turned down in Utah? FAIR plans, surplus lines, and DIC policies explained

What public sources say about where homes go when admitted insurers decline them.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published November 9, 2026. Sources checked November 9, 2026. See our editorial policy.

If your Utah home insurer non-renewed you and other companies are turning you down too, it helps to know how the market is set up, because Utah does not work the same way as states with a state-run backstop. This guide explains what public sources say about FAIR plans, surplus lines insurance, and difference in conditions policies in Utah. It does not tell you which to choose. If you have been dropped and want to talk with someone, our non-renewal page can refer you to a licensed Utah agent.

If you just received a notice, start with what happens after a Utah non-renewal notice, which covers the 30-day notice rule and your right to ask for the reason.

Does Utah have a FAIR plan?

A FAIR (Fair Access to Insurance Requirements) plan is an insurer of last resort for homeowners who cannot get conventional insurance. In a June 2026 presentation to a legislative interim committee, staff said 34 states have enacted a FAIR plan or similar policy, and that FAIR plans often provide dwelling-only or limited coverage for certain perils, with heightened eligibility requirements (OLRGC, Homeowners' Insurance Affordability, June 2026).

We did not find a FAIR plan in Utah law. A 2026 bill, H.B. 562, Homeowners Insurance Amendments, would have created an Access to Insurance Plan Association to run one, and would have required an applicant to show that at least three admitted insurers had declined coverage. H.B. 562 does not appear on the Legislature's list of bills passed in the 2026 General Session (checked November 9, 2026). Laws can change, so confirm the current situation with the Utah Insurance Department or a licensed agent.

Admitted insurers and surplus lines insurers

Most homeowners policies come from admitted insurers, which hold a certificate of authority to do business in Utah. The Department describes the surplus lines market as a supplemental market of insurers that do not have a certificate of authority in Utah. It says surplus lines laws require a diligent, good faith effort to place insurance with an admitted insurer first, and that all surplus lines transactions on Utah risks go to the Surplus Line Association of Utah for review and premium tax collection (Utah Insurance Department, Excess and Surplus Lines).

Under Utah Code 31A-15-103, insurance placed with a nonadmitted insurer must be placed by a surplus lines producer licensed under Utah's producer licensing chapter. You can check a Utah producer's license online.

The guaranty association difference

If an insurer fails, the Utah Property and Casualty Insurance Guaranty Association pays certain covered claims (Utah Code 31A-28-202). Under Utah Code 31A-28-203, a covered claim must involve an insolvent "member insurer," which the statute defines as an insurer licensed to transact insurance in Utah. Surplus lines insurers, as the Department describes them, are not authorized to do business in the state, so ask how a policy you are offered is treated.

Difference in conditions (DIC) policies

The Department says DIC policies can cover perils such as earthquake, flood, mudslide, and earth movement, and are typically written in the specialty (surplus) lines market, including through Lloyd's of London. It says these products differ from a typical home policy, recommends reading the exclusions and limitations before buying, suggests requesting a copy of the completed, signed application, and says the programs are available through independent agents (Utah Insurance Department, Difference In Condition Policies).

Questions to bring to an agent

  • Have admitted insurers declined my home, and why?
  • If a policy is offered, is the insurer admitted or nonadmitted, and who is the licensed surplus lines producer?
  • Which perils and limits does the policy include, and what does it exclude compared with my old policy?
  • Does my mortgage servicer accept this policy?
  • Is there anything about the home, such as roof or wildfire risk, that could change what admitted insurers will offer later?

If wildfire risk is the reason, see Utah's high-risk WUI boundary and the Summit County non-renewal data. If you have a dispute with an insurer, the Department has a complaint process.
