What is a fire protection class, and why does it matter in rural Utah?

How the 1 to 10 grade works, with Utah examples from public sources.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published November 2, 2026. Sources checked November 2, 2026. See our editorial policy.

A fire protection class is a 1 to 10 grade that Insurance Services Office (ISO), a company that supplies risk data to insurers, assigns to a community's fire protection, and insurers use it in underwriting and pricing. It can matter more in rural Utah, where homes may sit far from a fire station or a hydrant. This page explains how the grade works using public sources; it does not tell you what any insurer will charge. What the 1 to 10 scale means

ISO's Public Protection Classification (PPC) program grades communities from 1 to 10. Class 1 generally represents superior property fire protection, and Class 10 means the area's fire suppression program does not meet ISO's minimum criteria. ISO says it has information on more than 40,000 fire-response jurisdictions (ISO, PPC program).

A 2024 ISO summary report for the Hurricane Valley fire protection area in Washington County, posted on the Utah Public Notice Website, lays out the scoring (ISO PPC summary report, Hurricane Valley FPSA):

  • Emergency communications count for 10% of the score, the fire department for 50%, and the water supply for 40%, with up to 5.5 extra points for community risk reduction.
  • A score of 90 or more is Class 1, 60.00 to 69.99 is Class 4, and below 10 is Class 10.
  • Class 9 means a creditable dispatch center and fire department but no creditable water supply.
  • Class 10 applies to properties more than 5 road miles from a recognized fire station.

Why rural homes often get a split class

Several Utah fire districts have a split grade such as 5/5X. In the same ISO report, the first number applies to properties within 5 road miles of a recognized fire station and within 1,000 feet of a hydrant or other water supply; the "X" class applies to properties within 5 road miles but more than 1,000 feet from a hydrant. A separate "10W" class covers some properties 5 to 7 road miles from a station that have a recognized water supply within 1,000 feet (ISO PPC summary report).

Some Utah examples from public sources:

  • Hurricane Valley (Washington County): Class 04/4X, effective April 1, 2025, with 65.48 total points (ISO report). That total falls in the Class 4 band.
  • St. George: PPC 3/3X. The city says that out of 318 Utah communities surveyed, only 37 (about 11.6%, our calculation) had a PPC of 3 or better, and that neighborhoods 7 or more miles from a fire station are rated PPC 10 (City of St. George).
  • Summit, Morgan, and Wasatch counties: In 2024 KPCW reported that North Summit Fire District improved from 6/6X to 5/5X, and listed South Summit at 6/6X, Morgan County at 5/5X, Wasatch County at 4/4X, Park City at 3/3X, and the Salt Lake City Fire Department at 1. The station noted that many rural Utah fire departments rely on volunteers (KPCW, March 20, 2024).

Does the class change what you pay?

ISO's report says insurers use PPC information for marketing, underwriting, and setting premiums, and that fire insurance in a community with a good grade is generally substantially lower than in one with a poor grade, all other factors being equal (ISO PPC summary report). In the KPCW report, insurance agents said rates are more closely tied to the number of claims in an area than to ISO ratings, and that a better rating will not automatically lower rates (KPCW).

The Utah Insurance Department's 2025 homeowners comparison tables assume a 10-year-old dwelling in fire protection class 1 to 6 (Utah Insurance Department, 2025 comparison tables). So the sample prices there do not show what a home rated in class 7 through 10 might be charged.

How to find your class

  • Ask your agent or insurer which protection class they use for your address, and whether it is the first or second number of a split class.
  • Check your fire department or district. Some publish their grade, as St. George does.
  • Know your road distance to the nearest staffed fire station and whether a hydrant is within 1,000 feet; those are the distances the ISO report uses.
