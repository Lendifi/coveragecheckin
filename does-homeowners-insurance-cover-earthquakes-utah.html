Does homeowners insurance cover earthquakes in Utah?

Usually not. How earthquake coverage is offered in Utah, and what the separate deductible means.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published October 19, 2026. Sources checked October 19, 2026. See our editorial policy.

Usually not. The Utah Insurance Department says standard homeowners, condominium, and renters policies do not cover earthquake damage. Coverage is typically available as an endorsement added to your policy for an extra premium, or as a separate policy (Utah Insurance Department, 2024 comparison tables, page 6). For the hazard data behind the question, see Wasatch Front earthquake odds and your homeowners policy. To see whether earthquake coverage is already listed in your policy, see how to read your declarations page.

What the standard policy excludes

The department's glossary lists earthquake among the typical homeowners exclusions, along with sewer backup or sump pump failure, ordinance or law, and intentional loss. Its homeowners insurance page names flood, earthquake, earth movement, and mudslide as perils typically not covered, and says coverage for them can be obtained by endorsement or by separate policies. The NAIC also notes that flood and earthquake are typically excluded from homeowners policies (NAIC).

Three ways earthquake coverage is offered in Utah

  1. An endorsement on your homeowners policy. The department says many companies add earthquake coverage as an endorsement for additional premium (2024 comparison tables).
  2. A separate earthquake policy. The department says that if your company does not offer earthquake coverage, you may be able to get it through another company. In its 2024 tables, several of the large insurers listed were marked as not offering earthquake insurance (2024 comparison tables).
  3. A Difference in Conditions (DIC) policy. The department describes DIC policies as covering several perils, such as earthquake, flood, mudslide, and earth movement, usually written in the specialty (surplus) lines market, and available through independent insurance agents (Difference In Condition Policies). It notes that these products differ from a typical home policy, so their exclusions and limits are worth understanding before buying.

The separate deductible

Earthquake coverage has its own deductible, separate from your homeowners deductible (Utah Insurance Department). In the department's examples it is a percentage: in the 2024 earthquake table, the deductible was 10% of a $250,000 home, or $25,000. The department's 2025 examples also use a 10% deductible (2025 comparison tables). The NAIC notes that a deductible on a dec page may be a dollar amount or a percentage, and that questions about how it applies are for your agent or insurer (NAIC).

Landslides, flood, and other ground movement

  • Landslide. The department says landslides are not covered by homeowners policies, and that earthquake coverage covers landslides only if the earthquake triggered them. Otherwise landslide coverage is available only through a DIC policy or, in specific circumstances, earthquake coverage (2024 comparison tables, page 6).
  • Flood. Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. Flood coverage is a separate policy, available through an insurance professional or directly from the federal flood program at FloodSmart.gov, and the department notes there is usually a 30-day waiting period (2024 comparison tables).

What earthquake coverage costs varies a lot

The department's 2025 comparison tables list example earthquake premiums from 13 insurers for a hypothetical 10-year-old home with a 10% deductible in four ZIP codes. Southern Utah has its own active fault: the Utah Geological Survey lists the Hurricane fault among the state's most active, alongside the Wasatch fault (UGS, Utah Faults). The department describes these tables as a general baseline for shopping, not an endorsement and not a quote.

Questions people commonly ask an agent

  • Does my current insurer offer an earthquake endorsement, or would it be a separate policy?
  • What is the earthquake deductible, and what amount is the percentage applied to?
  • Does the coverage include personal property and loss of use, or only the dwelling?
  • Does my home's construction type, such as brick or frame, change the price?
