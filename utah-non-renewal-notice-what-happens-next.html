Got a non-renewal notice in Utah? What happens next

What the notice means, what Utah law requires, and the steps people commonly take.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published October 12, 2026. Sources checked October 12, 2026. See our editorial policy.

A non-renewal notice means your insurance company has told you it does not plan to renew your homeowners policy when the current term ends. It is not the same as a cancellation in the middle of a term, and Utah law treats the two differently. This guide walks through what Utah's insurance code says about non-renewal notices and what people commonly do next. If you have a notice in hand right now, our dropped or non-renewed page has a short checklist.

Non-renewals are not rare in Utah. In a December 2024 staff report, the U.S. Senate Budget Committee found that Utah's homeowners non-renewal rate rose from 0.72% in 2018 to 1.06% in 2023, an increase of about 47%, which ranked Utah 16th among the states for 2023 (Tables 5 and 7, pages 40 and 42 of the report). Those figures come from data insurers supplied to the committee and exclude condo and co-op policies.

If you live in the Wasatch Back, our page on Park City and Summit County non-renewals covers the county with the highest rate in the report's Utah data. If wildfire may be part of the reason, see how to check your Utah home's wildfire risk.

What Utah law says about the notice

Utah Code Section 31A-21-303 covers cancellation, issuance, and renewal for most property and casualty policies, including homeowners policies. A few points in the current text (effective May 7, 2025) are useful to know:

  • Timing. Your right to have the policy renewed ends if the insurer delivers or mails a notice of intention not to renew at least 30 days before the policy expires or reaches its anniversary date (Subsection 4(b)(i)).
  • The reason. If a notice of cancellation or non-renewal does not state with reasonable precision the facts behind the decision, the insurer must send that information within 10 working days after it receives your written request, and the notice is not effective unless it tells you about this right (Subsection 7).
  • Single weather claims. An insurer may not non-renew a homeowners policy solely because of one claim in a 36-month period for damage that came only from wind, hail, or lightning and was not preventable with reasonable care (Subsection 5(b)).
  • Renewal on worse terms. If the insurer offers to renew at higher rates or on less favorable terms, the new terms take effect on the renewal date only if you were sent notice at least 30 days before the old policy expires, with exceptions such as a rate increase that applies to the whole class of policies (Subsection 6).

If the reason is wildfire risk, a newer law adds more. Under Utah Code Section 31A-22-1310, in effect since January 1, 2026, an insurer that cancels or non-renews a wildland urban interface property because of wildfire risk must include the facts behind that decision in the notice, with reasonable precision.

Cancellation is different

A cancellation ends a policy before its term is up. Under the same section, an insurer generally may cancel mid-term only for nonpayment or for listed grounds such as material misrepresentation or a substantial change in the risk. Cancellation for reasons other than nonpayment takes effect no sooner than 30 days after written notice, and cancellation of a personal lines policy for nonpayment takes effect no sooner than 10 days after notice (Subsection 2). If your letter says "cancellation" rather than "non-renewal," the dates and rules can be different, so read it closely.

Steps people commonly take after a notice

  1. Find the end date. The notice states when coverage ends. That date sets your timeline.
  2. Read the stated reason. If the notice does not give the facts behind the decision, Utah law lets you ask for them in writing (Subsection 7 of 31A-21-303).
  3. Check your mortgage paperwork. Many home loans require continuous homeowners coverage. Your loan documents or servicer can tell you what yours requires.
  4. Talk with a licensed agent. An independent agent can look at options from more than one company. You can confirm any agent's Utah license with the Utah Insurance Department licensee search.
  5. Ask the state for help if something looks wrong. The Utah Insurance Department takes property and casualty complaints through its complaint page and says the process usually takes three to four weeks.
