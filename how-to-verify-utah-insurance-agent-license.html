How to verify a Utah insurance agent is licensed

Use the state's free Licensee Search to check status, lines of authority, and affiliations.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published October 26, 2026. Sources checked October 26, 2026. See our editorial policy.

Before you share personal details or pay for a policy, you can confirm that the person you are talking to holds an active Utah insurance license. The Utah Insurance Department runs a free public Licensee Search for finding and verifying agents and agencies (Utah Insurance Department, Licensee Search). If you have been dropped and are shopping for a new policy, our non-renewal page explains how we can refer you to a licensed Utah agent, and you can check that agent the same way.

If you are still working out what a non-renewal means, see what happens after a Utah non-renewal notice. For an example of the coverage questions you might bring to an agent, see St. George homes and what standard policies exclude.

Why the license details matter

  • Under Utah law, a license type and its lines of authority describe the lines of business a licensee may sell, solicit, or negotiate. Producer lines of authority include property, casualty, and personal lines insurance, along with others such as life and title (Utah Code 31A-23a-106).
  • Utah defines personal lines insurance as property and casualty coverage sold for primarily noncommercial purposes to an individual or a family (Utah Code 31A-1-301).
  • The Insurance Department says a producer may hold a license without an appointment, but may not solicit, negotiate, or bind coverage as an insurer's agent without an appointment from that insurer or a designation to an appointed agency (Utah Insurance Department, Licensees FAQ).
  • The department also says individual licenses renew every two years, at the end of the licensee's birth month, and that the Licensee Search is updated frequently throughout the day (Licensees FAQ).

Step by step: using the Licensee Search

This describes the tool as it appeared on October 26, 2026. Labels may change.

  1. Open the search. Start from the department's Licensee Search page, which links to the search tool. The department says that if the tool is down, or you are on a mobile device, you can use the Sircon license lookup instead.
  2. Pick a search type. The tool offers Search by Name, Search by License #, and Search by Location (a ZIP code, or a city and state, within a distance you choose from 1 to 75 miles). A "Show Only Active" setting filters out licenses that are not active.
  3. Read the results list. Each result shows a name, business address, business phone, license number, and status.
  4. Open the detail page. Selecting a name shows the license issue date and status, a Licenses table with each license and line of authority (with its status, expiration date, and number), and lists of active agency affiliations and active company affiliations.

What to compare

  • Status. Look for an active license.
  • Lines of authority. Check that the lines listed match the kind of insurance you are discussing. A homeowners policy is property and casualty coverage, so property, casualty, or personal lines are the relevant lines under the statutes above. A license with only an unrelated line, such as a title line, does not cover selling homeowners insurance.
  • Expiration date. Compare it to today's date.
  • Name, business address, and phone. Compare them to what the agent gave you.
  • Affiliations. The company affiliations list shows which insurers the producer is affiliated with, which relates to the appointment rule above. If the insurer named on your quote is not listed, that is a fair question to ask.

If something does not match

The state's flood insurance page gives a blunt version of the same advice: before signing an application or paying, call the Insurance Department and confirm the agent and company are licensed (Utah Flood Hazards, flood insurance). The department's phone numbers are (801) 957-9200 and toll free (800) 439-3805 (Utah Insurance Department). You can also file a complaint about an agent or insurer through its complaints page.
