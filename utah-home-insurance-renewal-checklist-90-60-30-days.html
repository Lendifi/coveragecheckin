Utah home insurance renewal checklist: 90, 60, and 30 days out

Which renewal dates come from Utah law, which come from mortgage rules, and a planning calendar for the rest.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published November 2, 2026. Sources checked November 2, 2026. See our editorial policy.

The Utah Insurance Department's sample homeowners policies run for a one-year term (2025 comparison tables), and Utah law sets a few dates that fall in the last weeks before that renewal. This guide lays out one way to use the three months before your renewal date, and marks which dates come from Utah law or federal mortgage rules and which are just a planning calendar. If you would like a reminder before your own renewal, our free renewal check can send one.

If you already have a non-renewal notice, start with what happens after a Utah non-renewal notice instead. To see what your current policy lists, see how to read your declarations page.

About 90 days out: gather what you have

  • Find your expiration date. It is on your declarations page, and every date below counts back from it.
  • Note what changed at home. The Utah Insurance Department suggests reviewing coverage every year with your agent and says changes in circumstances, property values, remodels, and additions may require adjustments to a policy (Utah Insurance Department, homeowners insurance).
  • Check how the dwelling is valued. The department explains the difference between replacement cost and actual cash value coverage on the same page.
  • Look up your wildfire map status. See how to check your Utah home's wildfire risk; since January 1, 2026, insurers deciding whether a property is high-risk wildland urban interface may only use the state's boundary (Utah Code 31A-22-1310; see our WUI explainer).

About 60 days out: get a sense of the market

  • Read the state's sample prices. The Insurance Department's comparison tables show what the largest insurers would charge for a standard profile ($450,000 dwelling, $1,000 deductible) in four sample ZIP codes, which change each year (Utah Insurance Department, 2025 comparison tables). They are a baseline, not a quote.
  • Talk with a licensed agent if you want other options. You can verify a Utah agent's license through the department's licensee search.
  • Ask about local factors. Your fire protection class is one; see what a fire protection class is.

45 to 14 days before the premium is due: the renewal bill

Under Utah Code Section 31A-21-303, an insurer can send a renewal premium notice no more than 45 and no less than 14 days before the renewal premium is due, stating the premium, how and when to pay it, and that not paying ends the right to renewal (Subsection 4(b)(ii)). To compare the new premium with the old one, our premium change calculator shows the dollar and percent change.

30 days out: two notice deadlines

  • Non-renewal. Your right to renewal ends if the insurer delivers or mails a notice of intention not to renew at least 30 days before the policy expires (Subsection 4(b)(i)).
  • Higher rates or less favorable terms. New terms or rates take effect on the renewal date if the insurer sent notice of them at least 30 days before the old policy expires. If it did not, they take effect 30 days after the notice, and you may cancel the renewal policy during that 30-day period (Subsection 6(a)). This does not apply when the only adverse change is, for example, a rate increase that applies to the whole class of policies (Subsection 6(b)).

Both rules are in Utah Code 31A-21-303 (current text effective May 7, 2025). A policy can give you more favorable terms than the statute requires (Subsection 1(b)).

If you have a mortgage

If your mortgage requires hazard insurance, your loan servicer tracks it. Under federal rule 12 CFR 1024.37, before a servicer charges you for insurance it buys on your behalf (force-placed insurance), it must send a first written notice at least 45 days before charging, then a reminder at least 30 days after the first notice and at least 15 days before charging. The notices must say that force-placed insurance may cost significantly more and may not provide as much coverage as insurance you buy. Within 15 days of receiving evidence that you had qualifying coverage, the servicer must cancel the force-placed policy and refund charges for any overlap. The notices ask the borrower to send the servicer their insurance information.

After renewal

  • Keep the new declarations page with last year's so you can see what changed.
  • Note the next expiration date. Our renewal check can remind you ahead of it.
