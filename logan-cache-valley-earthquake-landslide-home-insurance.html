Logan and Cache Valley homes: fault zones, the 1962 earthquake, and bluff landslides

Hazard facts from the Utah Geological Survey and National Weather Service, plus questions to ask an agent.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published October 28, 2026. Sources checked October 28, 2026. See our editorial policy.

Cache Valley sits between two of northern Utah's larger fault zones, had the costliest Utah earthquake of its time in 1962, and has had deadly landslides on the Logan bluffs. Earthquake, earth movement, and mudslide are all perils the Utah Insurance Department lists as typically not covered by a homeowners policy, along with flood (Utah Insurance Department, homeowners insurance). If you want time to ask about them before your policy renews, our free renewal check can send you a reminder ahead of that date.

The 1962 Cache Valley earthquake

According to a Utah Geological Survey photo essay on four historic Utah earthquakes, a magnitude 5.7 earthquake centered near Richmond struck at 6:35 a.m. on August 30, 1962 (UGS Public Information Series 72):

  • Major damage occurred in Richmond, Logan, and Lewiston. Masonry fell inside and outside buildings, windows shattered, and plaster dropped from ceilings.
  • Three-fourths of the houses in Richmond were damaged, and at least nine homes needed rebuilding.
  • In Logan, power was out for several hours and water lines were damaged. A contemporary newspaper account quoted by the UGS describes the roof of a Center Street business crashing to the floor.
  • A landslide in Logan Canyon covered part of US 89.
  • Damage was put at $1 million in 1962 dollars, which the 2001 UGS publication describes as the most costly earthquake in Utah to that date.

The East and West Cache fault zones

A 2024 UGS report describes the 40-mile-long East Cache fault zone and the 35-mile-long West Cache fault zone as bounding Cache Valley, with evidence of large surface-faulting earthquakes in both. It notes that Cache Valley's population grew 18% between 2010 and 2020, and that development is spreading along the valley margins and encroaching on these fault zones. Some of the East Cache fault zone's trench data come from the Logan Country Club golf course (UGS Report of Investigation 286). The UGS Utah Geologic Hazards Portal shows mapped faults near an address, and the UGS Utah faults page explains the state's fault data.

The Logan bluff landslides

On July 11, 2009, a landslide on the bluff below Utah State University in Logan destroyed a house and killed three people, and destroyed a section of the Logan Northern Canal. The UGS notes another landslide had occurred nearby on the same bluff in 2005 (UGS, the July 2009 Logan landslide). Earth movement and mudslide are on the Insurance Department's list of perils typically not covered (Utah Insurance Department); how a specific policy words those exclusions is a question for your agent or insurer.

Wind

In the September 8, 2020, downslope windstorm, the National Weather Service reported a peak gust of 68 mph at the Logan-Cache Airport (NWS Salt Lake City public information statement). Wind is not on the Insurance Department's list of typically excluded perils, but how your own policy treats wind damage, and which deductible applies, is shown in your policy.

Housing age

Logan's housing is newer than Salt Lake City's on average. Census Bureau estimates (2020 to 2024) put Logan's median year built at 1984, with 1,948 of about 19,588 housing units (9.9%) built in 1939 or earlier (ACS 2024 5-year, table B25034, via Census Reporter). We computed the percentage from those counts. Older brick buildings were among those damaged in 1962, so for older homes the construction type is worth knowing; for brick homes see our Salt Lake City brick homes guide.

Questions to bring to an agent

  • Does my policy include an earthquake endorsement, and what deductible applies to it?
  • How does my policy word the earth movement and mudslide exclusions, and would an earthquake-triggered landslide be treated differently?
  • Which deductible applies to wind damage on my policy?
  • Is my address near a mapped fault or landslide area, and does my insurer ask about that?
