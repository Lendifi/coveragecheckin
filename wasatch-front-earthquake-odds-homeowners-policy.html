Wasatch Front earthquake odds and your homeowners policy

The public data on the fault, the forecast, and earthquake premium examples for two Wasatch Front ZIP codes.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published October 19, 2026. Sources checked October 19, 2026. See our editorial policy.

The Wasatch fault zone, which the Utah Geological Survey calls one of the best studied continental normal-fault zones on Earth, runs along the Wasatch Front (UGS). State geologists estimate a 43% chance of at least one magnitude 6.75 or larger earthquake in the Wasatch Front region in the next 50 years. Standard homeowners policies generally do not cover earthquake damage, so this page brings together the public data for Wasatch Front homeowners. If your renewal is coming up, our renewal check can remind you ahead of time.

For the coverage side in more detail, see does homeowners insurance cover earthquakes in Utah? To find where earthquake coverage would appear in your own policy, see how to read your declarations page.

The forecast

The Utah Geological Survey (UGS) and the U.S. Geological Survey set up the Working Group on Utah Earthquake Probabilities in 2010. Its 2016 forecast (UGS), published as UGS Miscellaneous Publication 16-3, found these 50-year probabilities (UGS, Utah Earthquake Probabilities):

  • 43% for one or more earthquakes of magnitude 6.75 or larger in the Wasatch Front region.
  • 57% for one or more of magnitude 6.0 or larger in the region.
  • 18% for one or more of magnitude 6.75 or larger on the Wasatch fault zone itself.

The Utah Insurance Department's April 2016 summary of the study adds a 93% chance of a magnitude 5.0 earthquake in the same period (Utah Insurance Department).

The fault

The UGS lists the Wasatch fault along the Wasatch Front among the most active faults in Utah (Utah Faults). It describes the Wasatch fault zone as a series of faults in ten active segments along a 250-mile stretch, from north of Malad City, Idaho, to south of Fayette in central Utah (The Wasatch Fault from Above). The UGS Utah Faults page links to fault maps if you want to see where the mapped traces run near you.

A recent reminder: Magna, 2020

Early on March 18, 2020, a magnitude 5.7 earthquake centered about 8 miles below Magna, and about 10 miles west of downtown Salt Lake City, shook the Wasatch Front. There were no deaths, but injuries and damage to buildings and homes were reported. The UGS calls it the largest earthquake along the Wasatch Front since pioneer settlement in 1847 (UGS, September 2020).

Older brick homes

The Utah Seismic Safety Commission publishes a free guide, Putting Down Roots in Earthquake Country (PDF). A September 2022 UGS article on its second edition calls Utah's history of building homes and other buildings with unreinforced masonry, mostly brick, "a significant problem" for earthquakes, and says the guide explains how to identify that kind of construction (UGS Survey Notes).

The state's insurance price examples also differ by construction type. In the Utah Insurance Department's 2025 earthquake comparison, 11 of the 13 insurers listed show a higher example premium for a brick home than for a frame home in each of the four sample ZIP codes (2025 comparison tables). The tables do not give reasons for the differences.
