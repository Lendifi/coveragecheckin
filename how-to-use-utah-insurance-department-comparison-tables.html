How to use the Utah Insurance Department comparison tables

What the state's sample premiums show, what they leave out, and how to line them up with your renewal.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published November 23, 2026. Sources checked November 23, 2026. See our editorial policy.

Every year the Utah Insurance Department publishes comparison tables with sample home insurance premiums from the largest insurers in the state. They are free and official, but easy to misread. This guide explains what the tables show, what they leave out, and how to line them up against your own renewal. Before your renewal arrives, set a free renewal check reminder, and use our premium change calculator to measure your own change in dollars and percent.

Why the tables exist

Utah Code 31A-2-208.5 requires the insurance commissioner to publish a yearly table comparing homeowners and auto rates for the top 20 insurers by premium volume, plus others that ask to be included, using at least four hypothetical examples. The same law requires a table of each insurer's ratio of confirmed complaints to premium and its combined loss and expense ratio.

What the 2025 homeowners tables assume

The 2025 comparison tables use one standardized profile:

  • A 10-year-old dwelling in fire protection class 1 to 6 (see what a fire protection class is).
  • For a standard HO 3 policy: $450,000 dwelling, $300,000 personal liability, $1,000 medical payments, and a $1,000 deductible (the department notes one insurer's deductible is $2,500).
  • Renters (HO 4) with $50,000 contents, and condo unit (HO 6) with $100,000 contents.
  • No special rates or promotional discounts, and a one-year term.

The 2025 sample ZIP codes are Ogden (84403), Taylorsville (84123), Fillmore (84631), and Hurricane (84737). The department says it changes the ZIP codes each year to represent different parts of the state; the 2024 edition used Holladay (84117), Farr West (84404), St. George (84770), and Price (84501) (2024 comparison tables). So a year-over-year comparison for the same town is usually not possible from the tables alone.

Reading the columns

  • Brick and frame HO 3 prices. Separate sample premiums for a brick home and a wood frame home with the same limits.
  • NA. The department says NA means that policy type is not offered through that insurer, and that coverage might be available through an affiliate.
  • Earned premium. A dollar figure for each insurer. The department uses it as the base for the complaint ratio, so it reflects how much business the insurer writes, not a price.
  • Complaint ratio. Valid consumer complaints per $100,000 of earned premium. The 2024 edition gives a worked example: a company with 10 valid complaints and $20,000,000 of earned premium has a ratio of 0.0500 (2024 comparison tables). A ratio lets large and small insurers be compared on the same scale.
  • Loss and expense ratio. Claims paid plus administrative expenses, compared to premiums. The 2024 edition explains that a ratio above 1.000 means the company paid out more than it collected; 1.150 means $1.15 out for every $1.00 in.

What the tables do not tell you

  • Your price. The 2024 edition says premiums vary with the home's age, location, and condition, credit information, discounts, surcharges, and added coverages, and that the list is not a recommendation by the department (2024 comparison tables). The 2025 page carries a similar disclaimer (2025 comparison tables).
  • Coverage differences. The department cautions that price is not the only factor and points to service, financial stability, and available coverages. Two policies with the same limits can differ in endorsements, exclusions, and how claims are settled.
  • Excluded perils. The 2024 edition notes that standard policies do not cover earthquake, flood, or sewer backup. Earthquake has its own table; see does homeowners insurance cover earthquakes in Utah? and does homeowners insurance cover sewer backup?
  • Switching costs. The 2024 edition notes that changing companies mid-term can bring early cancellation charges and suggests checking with your current company about amounts owed.

Lining the tables up with your renewal

  1. Pull your declarations page and note your dwelling limit, liability limit, and deductible. Our declarations page guide shows where each one is.
  2. Compare them with the table's profile ($450,000 dwelling, $300,000 liability, $1,000 deductible). The further apart they are, the less the sample prices say about your situation.
  3. Note whether your home is brick or frame and which sample ZIP is most similar to where you live; the 2024 edition suggests looking at the ZIP most like yours.
  4. Measure your own renewal change with the premium change calculator, and read why Utah renewals go up for the public factors behind increases.
