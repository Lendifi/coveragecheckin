Does homeowners insurance cover sewer backup in Utah?

Sewer backup coverage, sewer line responsibility, and claim rules, from the Utah Insurance Department, Utah cities, and Utah law.

By the CoverageCheckin editorial team. Published November 23, 2026. Sources checked November 23, 2026. See our editorial policy.

Usually not, unless the coverage was added. The Utah Insurance Department lists sewer backup and sump pump failure among typical homeowners exclusions, and says coverage is available for an additional premium. This page explains what public sources say about that coverage, who is responsible for the sewer line, and what Utah law says about claims against a city or district. To check your own policy before it renews, look at the endorsements on your declarations page and set a free renewal check reminder.

What the Utah Insurance Department says

The department's glossary lists earthquake, sewer backup or sump pump failure, ordinance or law, and intentional loss as typical homeowners exclusions. It describes sewer back-up and sump pump overflow coverage as coverage available for an additional premium that pays for damage caused by sump pump failure or sewer back-up, and notes that water seepage is not covered (Utah Insurance Department, Glossary of Homeowner Insurance Terms). Its 2024 comparison tables say sewer back up is not covered under the standard policy and can be added by an endorsement (2024 comparison tables).

The NAIC's consumer guide says the same nationally: most homeowners policies offer limited or no coverage for sewer or drain backups and sump pump overflow, and you might be able to buy an endorsement for them (NAIC, A Consumer's Guide to Home Insurance). Our declarations page guide explains where endorsements are listed.

Sewer backup is not the same as flood

Flood is a separate exclusion with a separate kind of policy; the NAIC says the National Flood Insurance Program writes most flood insurance policies (NAIC consumer guide). Our article on flood coverage in Utah covers that. The Utah Insurance Department also says interior water damage from a storm, when there is no damage to the roof or walls, is not covered under most standard policies (Utah Insurance Department, Home Claims Information).

Who owns the sewer line

The pipe from your house to the public sewer main is called the lateral. In the two Utah examples below, the city or district maintains the main and the property owner is responsible for the lateral:

  • Ogden. Ogden City says the property owner is responsible for maintenance and repair of the sewer lateral from the home to the city main, including the tap connection, and that only a licensed contractor can work in the city right-of-way. It says a significant portion of Ogden homes built between 1900 and 1950 have a shared lateral serving two or more houses, which is no longer allowed in new construction (Ogden City, Sewer Utility Information). The city also notes that many homeowners policies do not cover the cost of repairing or replacing utility lines, which is a separate question from backup damage inside the home.
  • Jordan Basin Improvement District. The district's sewer backup page says the owner pays to fix a stoppage in the private line, and that the district can only be held liable for damage if it knew of a problem in its system and did not fix it in a reasonable time. It adds that sewer backups are not automatically covered by all homeowner's policies (Jordan Basin Improvement District, Sewer Backups).

Ogden explains that when a city line backs up, wastewater can come out of the lowest opening, which in homes with basements can be lower drains and toilets (Ogden City, sewer backup action steps).

If you think the city or district caused it

Utah's Governmental Immunity Act (Title 63G, Chapter 7) sets a notice rule for claims against a governmental entity. Under Utah Code 63G-7-402, a claim against a governmental entity is barred unless a notice of claim is filed according to Utah Code 63G-7-401 within one year after the claim arises. Section 401 sets out what the notice must contain and where it goes. We are not lawyers and do not interpret how these rules apply to any situation.

After a backup

The Utah Insurance Department's claims page lists general steps after damage: contact your insurer or agent as soon as possible, take photos or video, make repairs needed to prevent further damage but hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer inspects, and keep receipts (Utah Insurance Department, Home Claims Information). Questions to bring to your insurer or an agent

  • Does my policy include water backup of sewers or drains or sump overflow, and what is the limit and deductible?
  • Can it be added mid-term, or only at renewal?
  • How does my policy treat seepage, groundwater, and flood compared with backup?
  • Does my policy cover any repair to the sewer lateral itself?
